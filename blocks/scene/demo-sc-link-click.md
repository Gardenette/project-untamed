The names of characters in this scene are *more&#x20;*&#x74;han just names. They are linked to *Actants*.

*Actants&#x20;*&#x61;re the named things that make up your story. Your characters, locations, symbols, important items, factions, historical events, or really anything that you determine is important enough to give a name can be created as an *Actant* in the `Worldbuilding` domain.

## Actant Classes

*Actants&#x20;*&#x61;re generic blocks of text that are categorized by their *Classes*. There are 7 block types available in the Worldbuilding domain of any new Foma project: 2 organizational and 5 creative.

### Organizational Blocks

Organizational Blocks exist to help you categorize your creative *Actants* and assign information to them. They consist of Notes and Groups.

![image-84.png](assets/image-84.png)

Use Groups to gather like-*Actants*. For example, in the `Worldbuilding` domain of this project, Groups are used to organize Characters, Locations, Objects, and Story notes:

![image-85.png](assets/image-85.png)

Characters are nested under a Group named "Characters".

### Creative Actant Classes

Creative *Actant* Classes are the basic building blocks of story. Foma projects have built-in Creative *Actant* Classes for Characters, Locations, Items, Concepts, and Quests.

### Custom Actant Classes

Your story may contain *Actant* classes that Foma does not support natively. That's okay: Foma allows you to create custom *Actant* classes to suit your needs. To access the Custom *Actant* Class builder, click the "Manage Actant Classes" icon next to the Worldbuilding domain in the side bar.

![image-88.png](assets/image-88.png)

Click this to create custom Actant classes

To create a custom *Actant* class, type the name.

![image-90.png](assets/image-90.png)

Then, select the icon that best represents your *Actant* class.

![image-91.png](assets/image-91.png)

Once you've created your new *Actant* class, create new instances of it by clicking the `+` next to the Worldbuilding domain or assign it as the default *Actant* class of an existing *Actant*.

## Templates

As you write your character sheets or other *Actant* blocks, you may find that you're re-writing a lot of common structures. If you find that your character sheets follow the same structure each time you create a new one, consider creating a *Template&#x20;*&#x69;nstead. Foma comes pre-loaded with several basic *Templates&#x20;*&#x66;or various *Actant* classes, but you may find that you prefer to create your own.

To access the Template Library, click the Template Library icon in the upper-left corner of the title bar:

![image-95.png](assets/image-95.png)

Template Library

The Template Library allows you to create new Templates or copy existing templates to modify for your own purposes.

![image-94.png](assets/image-94.png)

The Template Library lets you inspect existing templates, create new, or create copies to modify

### Creating Templates from Existing Actants

You may find that, during the course of creating a Character Page, that you like the layout that you've created and you'd like to turn that character page into a Template, but stripped of information that is specific to the *Actant* that you've already written.

To turn an existing *Actant* page into a template, simply type `/template` while you are typing in the *Actant*'s page to create a template based on that *Actant*.

Try this:

Navigate to the Character *Actant* under `Worldbuilding → Characters → Professor `Gregory. In an empty line, type `/template` and select `Save as Template`:

![image-96.png](assets/image-96.png)

Then, fill out the "Save as Template" form.

![image-97.png](assets/image-97.png)

💬

*NOTE: the Scope of your new Template defines whether the Template is available to the current project, or across ALL projects on your machine. To make a Template available to ALL projects, set the Scope to "Global".*
