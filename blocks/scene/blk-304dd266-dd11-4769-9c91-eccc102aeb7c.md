Every scene you have written in this tutorial happened on a draft — you just never had to think about it. Look at the teal pill in the title bar, the one with the branch icon: it reads `draft-0`. That is the draft you are on, and every Foma project begins its life there.

![image-120.png](assets/image-120.png)

A draft is a complete, parallel version of your entire project — manuscript, worldbuilding, threads, all of it. Where memories let you travel back in time, drafts let you travel sideways: try the other ending, gut a chapter, rewrite the whole second act, all while `draft-0` sits untouched exactly as you left it.

***

### Creating Drafts

Click the draft pill to open the `Drafts` menu. It lists every draft in the project, with a checkmark beside the one you are on, and offers two ways to make another:

**New Draft** — begins as a full copy of everything on your current draft. This is the one you will reach for most: name it (Foma suggests the shape — `revision-1` — and tidies whatever you type into lowercase-with-dashes), press `Create`, and you are standing in a perfect duplicate, free to break things.

**New Empty Draft** — begins blank. For the from-scratch rewrite, the outline experiment, the version where you finally tell the story in first person.

New drafts copy the draft you are *currently on*, so drafts can be based on other drafts: to branch off `revision-1`, switch to `revision-1` first, then click **New Draft**. Draft lineage is yours to shape.

***

### Switching Between Drafts

Click any draft in the `Drafts` menu to switch to it. The whole project swaps to that draft's state — side bar, scenes, worldbuilding, everything — and the pill updates to show where you are. Switch back and your other version is waiting in the same state that it was in previously.

The `Loremaster` travels with you: memories you create on a draft belong to that draft's history, so each version of your story keeps its own past.

💬

*NOTE: Switching is always safe — Foma takes an automatic snapshot of your work before every switch, so nothing you typed can be left behind. Still, a named memory before major draft surgery gives Compare (below) a clean point to measure from.*

***

### Comparing Drafts

Two drafts eventually raise a question: *what actually changed?* Type `/compare` to open `Compare Drafts`. Pick a draft on the `Left`, a draft on the `Right`, and click `Compare`.

The verdict arrives in plain sentences — what the right draft edited, added, renamed, or removed compared to the left — followed by a document-by-document breakdown, scene titles first, with the exact changed passages beneath.

When you find that you've written something that you like in one draft, but you'd like to apply it to another, you do not have to copy-paste it home. When the left side is the draft you are standing on, changed scenes from the right side offer a `Bring this over` button — click it, and that scene crosses from the experimental draft into your current one, marked `brought over` when it lands.

💬

*NOTE: Comparisons read saved memories, not unsaved keystrokes. If a comparison looks stale, create a memory on the drafts involved and compare again.*

***

### Drafts and Co-writing

Importantly, in the current iteration of Foma, **only&#x20;**`draft-0`**&#x20;can be co-written**. Syncing, shared comments, and Review Decisions all live on `draft-0` — it is the draft you and your co-writers hold in common. Every other draft is your private workspace: it stays on your machine and never syncs to anyone.

This restriction was put in place for the co-writing feature's initial release to minimize complexity and risk of data loss. In the future, Foma will support co-writing across branches.

That said, for now, treat this restriction as part of its own workflow. Experiment alone on a side draft, as wildly as you like. When something earns its place in the shared story, switch to `draft-0`, `/compare` it against your experiment, and `Bring this over` only for the keepers. Your co-writers see your best idea — not the forty attempts behind it.

***

Try this: click the draft pill, create a **New Draft** named `experiment`, and rewrite Jane's line in "Pay it off." Create a memory, switch back to `draft-0`, then `/compare` the two drafts and bring your better line home.

And that completes the Git for Writers tour. Your work saves itself, remembers itself, backs itself up, shares itself — and now it can split into as many parallel possibilities as you care to imagine. Where you take it from here is, as ever, your story.
