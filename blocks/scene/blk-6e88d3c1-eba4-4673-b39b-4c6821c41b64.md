Writing is usually solitary. It does not have to be. Foma lets two (or more) writers work on the same project from their own computers, each holding a full copy of the manuscript — no extra accounts, no servers, no subscription. The GitHub connection you made at the start of this chapter is the entire infrastructure.

A co-writer needs the same two things you have: Foma, and a free GitHub account.

***

### Invite a Co-writer

Type `/share` to open the `Share Project` window. (It also lives in the `Archive` menu, behind `Invite collaborators`.) Sharing sits on top of the Archive, so the project must already be linked to a GitHub repository — the first section of this chapter covered that.

![image-104.png](assets/image-104.png)

Search for co-writers through the GitHub username search feature

Start typing in `Invite by GitHub username…` and Foma looks the writer up as you type, showing their avatar and name so you can confirm you have the right person before anything is sent. Click `Invite`.

![image-105.png](assets/image-105.png)

Click "Invite" when you've found your co-writer by search

Once your co-writer accepts, they appear in this window's collaborator list. The same window is where you revoke a pending invitation or remove a collaborator later — removal takes away their access to the shared GitHub project space, though they keep whatever local copy they have already downloaded.

💬

*NOTE: An invitation grants full writing access. This is a co-author's seat, not a reader's — invite writers you trust with the manuscript.*

***

### Join a Shared Project

When someone invites you, Foma notifies you in three places: behind the bell on the welcome screen, under `My collaborations` in `Open From Archive`, and on a bell in the title bar while you are writing. Type `/join` to jump straight to your invitations from anywhere.

Each invitation names the writer who sent it and the exact repository you are being invited into. Click `Accept`, and Foma downloads the project for you. From the welcome screen it opens immediately; if you are in the middle of writing something else, Foma asks whether to `Clone now`, `Clone & switch`, or `Later` — an invitation never interrupts a writing session.

💬

*NOTE: "Cloning" is GitHub's way of expressing that it is creating a local copy of a project that lives on the server. It just means that a copy will be downloaded to your computer from the remote location.*

***

### Writing Together

Day to day, co-writing feels exactly like writing alone: draft, remember, `Sync to Archive`. Every sync downloads your co-writer's latest work first, then uploads yours, and Foma weaves the two together.

When a co-writer has pushed work you have not seen yet, a small dot appears on the GitHub icon in the title bar. Sync to pull their writing in.

💬

*NOTE: Foma merges at the level of blocks and paragraphs, not whole files. You can both write in the same scene at the same time, and as long as you are not rewriting the very same paragraph, both contributions land cleanly side by side.*

***

### Settle Review Decisions

So what happens when you *do* both rewrite the very same paragraph? Nothing dramatic. Foma keeps one version in the text so writing never stops, parks both versions for review, and shows a count in the title bar. Type `/decisions` to open `Review Decisions`.

![image-106.png](assets/image-106.png)

Review which versions to keep

Each open decision is a plain-language card — "You both revised the same paragraph in *Chapter Three*" — showing each writer's version with their name attached. Click `Keep this version` on either one, `Keep both` to keep both passages in the text, or `Keep as it is` to bless the choice Foma already made. `Keep all as they are` clears the whole list at once.

💬

*NOTE: No decision here can destroy writing. Whichever you keep, both versions remain in the project's history, and the Loremaster remembers how to find them.*

***

### Comments You Can Share

Comments (`/comments`, Ctrl+Alt+M) are *private&#x20;*&#x74;o you by default. To bring one into the open, hover it in the Comments panel and click the share icon — the comment moves into the project's shared channel, where your co-writers can read it, reply to it, and resolve it after their next sync. Your GitHub avatar rides along, so everyone knows which margin notes belong to whom.

A small chip beside the scene title tells you when the open scene has shared comments waiting, and it stays accent-colored until you have read them. Private comments stay private unless you explicitly share them.

***

### See Who Did What

Type `/activity` for a feed of recent work in plain sentences — "Alex edited 'The Storm Breaks' — 12m ago" — newest first, grouped by day. The feed is a window, not a control panel: it never changes the project. It just keeps you from being surprised by it.

***

Try this: type `/share` and look up a friend's GitHub username — you do not have to click `Invite`. If you would rather rehearse the whole loop for real, this demo project is the perfect sandbox: invite a trusted friend, write over each other on purpose, and settle the decisions together. Nothing precious is at stake here.
