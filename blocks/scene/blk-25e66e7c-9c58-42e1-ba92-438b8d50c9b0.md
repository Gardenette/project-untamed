Think of Git as an automatic, ultra-reliable time machine for your writing, and GitHub is a secure cloud-based vault that holds your Git-backed work.

### How it works

Whenever you write, Foma uses Git under the hood to capture precise snapshots of your progress. Rather than forcing you to juggle file names like `Chapter_1_v2_final.md` or worrying about losing an earlier draft, Git tracks every phrase that you craft, rename, or delete across your entire project. Foma groups your snapshots into units called *memories&#x20;*&#x73;o that you aren't saving loose pages. With Git, you're preserving complete units of creative work that you can look back on and manage later.

If you ever want to explore creative choices and directions that you made earlier, you can review them in the `Loremaster's Memory` menu, which is accessible in the upper-right corner of the title bar:

![image-58.png](assets/image-58.png)

Git allows you to instantly roll back your manuscript's content and structure to any previous state from the `Loremaster's Memory` menu. More on this later.

### Saving Memories to GitHub

GitHub takes that local Git-backed safety net and connects it to the cloud. By syncing Foma with your GitHub account, your memories are continuously backed up to a private, remote server. *This means that your manuscript remains completely safe from device crashes, is accessible across all of your Foma-compatible devices, and is fully protected without requiring you to manage complex technical commands or worry about overwriting or misplacing your work.*
